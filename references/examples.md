# Additional fictional contrasts

These examples teach decisions. Every person, company, capability and result is invented for the stated input. Use them to learn the method, never as evidence for a real email.

## 1. Existing usage changes the asset

**Fictional inputs:** Imani leads support at ParcelNest. Her own post says the team already uses ReplyWorks for delivery-status questions and is preparing a returns workflow. The published returns policy permits self-service returns for unopened items and sends damaged-item claims to an agent. ReplyWorks documentation supports answers from policies and handover to an agent. A fictional customer story says WrenShop used ReplyWorks to resolve unopened-item returns automatically while preserving agent handling for damaged-item claims. The sender can prepare illustrative conversations from the public policy. These are invented teaching facts, not real customer evidence.

**Weak:**

> Imani, with ParcelNest growing, support tickets can pile up. ReplyWorks uses AI to reduce repetitive questions. Want a guide to launching your first support bot?

The email invents ticket pressure and ignores the adoption already described in the source.

**Stronger:**

> Subject: the returns handover
>
> Hi Imani,
>
> Saw you're adding returns to ParcelNest's ReplyWorks setup.
>
> We help retailers resolve routine returns without pulling agents into every conversation. WrenShop used ReplyWorks to automate unopened-item returns while keeping damaged-item claims with agents.
>
> Worth sending two sample conversations based on ParcelNest's policy to show that handover?

**Why it works:** The next rollout determines the relevant seller outcome. The customer result demonstrates the same boundary, and the asset makes it tangible in ParcelNest's context. The email acknowledges existing usage and makes no claim to know the current configuration.

**Transfer boundary:** If only product usage is known and no next initiative is evidenced, do not invent a returns rollout. Research an actual relevant task or use an honest proposed example with its uncertainty preserved.

## 2. Missing-proof fallback: retain the outcome and disclose the gap

**Inputs:** Arun leads training at FieldRoute. Public onboarding documentation shows that new installers complete three prerequisite modules before booking a supervised visit. SkillSlate documentation supports branching practice exercises. No launch date, internal difficulty or customer case is known. The sender can create a small sample exercise from the public onboarding steps.

**Weak:**

> As you scale your installer network, inconsistent onboarding is probably slowing productivity. SkillSlate streamlines training with an all-in-one platform. Can I send a personalized best-practices guide?

None of the supposed growth or onboarding problems is in the evidence. The guide has a personalized label without a useful proposition.

**Fallback after unsuccessful proof research:**

> Subject: before the supervised visit
>
> Hi Arun,
>
> Saw FieldRoute's installers complete three modules before a supervised visit.
>
> We help field-service teams prepare installers for site visits through SkillSlate's branching practice exercises.
>
> Worth sending a sample based on one of your onboarding steps, showing what an installer would choose and what happens next?

**What it preserves and what remains missing:** An observable process determines the sample, and the seller's contribution is still explicit. No “why now” is fabricated. There is no customer result to support the outcome, so `note` must say `missing_customer_proof`. This is a usable fallback when the caller permits one, not the full-quality default. Continue to prefer the complete signal, outcome, proof and asset structure when evidence supports it.

**Transfer boundary:** A vague statement that the company trains installers would not support the three modules or supervised visit. The skill should search for a real work object before settling for category copy.

## 3. Specific-looking offers can still be vague

**Inputs:** Lila leads event operations at Cedar Forum. The public agenda has two simultaneous workshops, and the ticket form currently asks attendees to choose one. SessionNest documentation supports workshop preferences and separate session confirmations. The sender can sketch a sample attendee journey using the public event and sample data. No information about Cedar's internal systems or problems is available.

**Plausible weak offer:**

> Can I send a personalized workshop operations blueprint covering registration architecture, attendance orchestration and cross-functional governance?

The technical nouns do not reveal the useful work. The offer also suggests access and implementation work the sender has not established.

**Better offer:**

> I can sketch the signup and confirmation an attendee would see when choosing between Cedar's two workshops. Want the example?

This is an offer fragment, not a complete email. The surrounding message still needs the specific signal, seller outcome and relevant customer proof, or an explicitly disclosed proof gap. The asset is small enough to inspect and feasible from public material. It does not promise to improve conversion or diagnose a broken registration process.

**Another weak repair:**

> Want a one-page guide to improving workshop registration?

Shortening the blueprint removed the jargon but also removed the specific work. The correct repair is to choose the concrete example, then describe it simply.

## 4. Thin input is not permission to invent a clever diagnosis

**Inputs:** Eva is a newly appointed operations director at a 300-person services company. The seller provides scheduling software. Nothing identifies Eva's remit, scheduling process, current tools or active initiatives.

**Tempting draft:**

> Eva, joining a 300-person team usually means untangling scheduling bottlenecks. I can send a staffing-efficiency scorecard tailored to your first 90 days. Worth sending?

It has a personal event and an asset-shaped CTA. It still invents the problem, assumes the remit and disguises a generic document as tailored help.

**Correct next move:** Research whether the firm schedules field visits, client sessions, shift coverage or some other supported work, and whether Eva owns it. Inspect a public process or relevant professional statement. The discovered work should determine what the sample contains.

If that research establishes no relevant work, the honest result remains limited. Do not claim high personalization because the draft contains Eva's appointment. A brief gap note is more informative than an unsupported diagnosis. If the caller still wants a draft, make its role-level basis explicit outside the copy and offer only a supported, clearly described sample.

## Reading these examples

Transfer the complete argument: signal, seller outcome, customer result and asset CTA. Wording can vary while retaining those functions. A different brief may call for a comparison, an annotated finding, a small set of examples or another bounded asset. Its value must be visible to the actual recipient and connected to what the seller helps achieve.
