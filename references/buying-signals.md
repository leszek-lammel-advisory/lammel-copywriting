# Finding a useful buying signal

Read [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) for the complete method. This file adds examples of search and judgment. All companies, roles, source excerpts and situations below are fictional teaching inputs. They are not prospect research or customer proof.

## Build the search from the work

Before searching, name the exact workflow your product helps change. Then choose the roles and language most likely to reveal that change. These are starting points; verify the seller's actual capabilities.

| Use case | Relevant work and role families | Evidence worth investigating | Tempting false positive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spend and finance operations | Accounts payable, expenses, procurement, reconciliation. Controller, Accounting Manager, Finance Systems, Procurement. | A mandate to automate invoice processing, integrate finance systems or redesign purchase approvals. | A payroll vacancy with routine duties, or a funding announcement with no finance initiative. |
| Support automation | Ticket resolution, self-service, knowledge and escalation. Support Operations, Head of Support, Customer Experience. | A stated project to improve self-service, automate repeat requests or redesign support routing. | A renewals-focused Customer Success Manager or internal IT support role. |
| Design collaboration | Shared components, product consistency, designer-to-developer handoff. Design Systems, DesignOps, Product Design, Design Engineer. | A project to unify libraries across products or improve a named handoff process. | “Proficient in design tools,” or an API launch with no relevant UX work. |
| Embedded payments | A platform helps its business customers collect money and receive payouts. Payments Product, platform engineering, VP Product. | Launching merchant onboarding, payout capabilities or payments inside the customer's workflow. | Collecting the platform's own SaaS subscription fees. |
| Developer tooling | Development, review and delivery workflows. Developer Experience, Engineering Productivity, Platform Engineering. | A stated developer-tool evaluation, migration or improvement program. | General engineering hiring or a programming language in the requirements. |

The source must explain the connection. International expansion alone is weak for a payments pitch. An announced need to support local payment methods for a new merchant market is much closer to the work.

## A short Google search sequence

Replace the bracketed fields with actual names and words. Run focused queries, inspect the results, then choose the next query. You do not need to run the whole list.

1. **Understand the product:** `site:[seller-domain] "[product name]"`. Confirm what it does and for whom.
2. **Find the work:** `site:[company-domain] "[workflow phrase]"`. Try a change verb or synonymous phrase from the product and company pages.
3. **Find an active mandate:** `"[company name]" "[relevant role]" jobs`. Open the official careers site and follow its link to any external hiring platform. Read responsibilities and employer attribution.
4. **Find the owner:** `"[person name]" "[company name]" "[initiative]"`. Check that the role and statement concern the current employer. A company project does not prove personal ownership.
5. **Check timing or contradiction:** `"[company name]" "[initiative]" after:[YYYY-MM-DD]`. Look for launches, completion announcements or a more recent change of direction. Inspect actual event dates.
6. **Find relevant proof:** `site:[seller-domain] "[workflow phrase]" "customer"`. Read the complete story and preserve the exact product, result and conditions.

Use quotes sparingly. A long exact phrase can miss equivalent wording. If the first search fails, try a shorter phrase, a related role or a different source type. A company blog and a job description often use different language for the same project. Use the company's working language where useful.

An unindexed or inaccessible page is a coverage gap. Do not turn a search snippet into a verified quotation, or a lack of results into evidence that the company has no initiative.

## Example 1: read the mandate, not the tool keyword

**Brief:** A design-collaboration product that supports shared component libraries. The target is BrightBench's current Head of Product Design.

**Candidate evidence:**

- BrightBench's careers page links to an open Design Systems Lead role. Its responsibilities include “unify the web and mobile component libraries before the new mobile experience launches.”
- Another open role says “experience using design and prototyping tools.”
- A company announcement introduces a new mobile experience. It does not name the Head of Product Design as project owner.

**Search progression:**

```text
"BrightBench" "Design Systems" jobs
"BrightBench" "component library"
"BrightBench" "mobile experience"
```

**Judgment:** The unification mandate is the strongest signal. The mobile announcement supports its context. Generic tool proficiency is background. The contact's current function makes them relevant, but there is no evidence that they personally lead the project.

**What to say:** “Saw BrightBench is unifying its web and mobile component libraries.”

**What to offer:** A small example of how one component could work across both surfaces, if the seller supports that approach. Research a customer result about library reuse or the same workflow before drafting the commercial middle.

**What not to infer:** The current library is broken, the team has chosen to replace a vendor, or the recipient personally commissioned the vacancy. The announcement and vacancy may describe the same initiative; they are corroboration, not two independent buying signals.

## Example 2: the right keyword can describe the wrong money flow

**Brief:** A payments product for software platforms whose customers need to collect money. ClassNest sells course-management software to schools.

**Candidate evidence:**

- An Accounting Specialist vacancy covers reconciling monthly subscription invoices that ClassNest sends to schools.
- An open Product Manager role describes adding student checkout and payouts to schools inside ClassNest.
- A profile mentions a finance transformation led by the target contact at a previous employer.

**Search progression:**

```text
"ClassNest" "payments" jobs
"ClassNest" "student checkout"
"ClassNest" "payouts"
```

**Judgment:** The product role describes the relevant initiative: student pays school through ClassNest. ClassNest billing schools for its own software is a different use case. The former-employer project is experience, not a current mandate.

**Recipient:** Find the current product or payments owner. Do not assume an adjacent finance employee owns the integration simply because the account is relevant.

**Asset direction:** A sample journey showing student payment, the platform fee and the school's payout, using public context and sample data. The proof must concern embedded payments or payouts, not a result from managing a vendor's own SaaS subscriptions.

## Example 3: a sourceable supporting signal beats a fabricated strong one

**Brief:** Support automation for software companies. Trailnote has an open customer-facing Support Specialist vacancy. The role handles tickets and troubleshooting; it names no automation initiative.

Research also finds 80,000 community members in an older article and 120,000 downloads in a newer article. The new VP of Customer Success's responsibilities cover renewals and account expansion, with no stated support ownership.

**Judgment:** The vacancy shows current investment in frontline support capacity. It does not prove a backlog, a hiring surge, an automation project or intent to buy. Community members and downloads cannot establish like-for-like user growth. The VP's title does not resolve support ownership.

**Next searches:** Look for the actual support owner and a public support workflow, such as a help-center article or a product-specific escalation route. If these reveal a concrete task, a small worked example for that task can be useful without invented urgency. If they do not, acknowledge the limited basis for personalization.

Do not write “As exploding user growth overwhelms your support team.” None of those claims follows from the evidence.

## Carry the useful evidence into the email

A research handoff should preserve the fact, current source, date or status, company attribution, owner if known, and uncertainty. Do not compress everything to “high-intent account,” which throws away the material the writer needs.

Choose the strongest supported work fact for the opener. Use the remaining research to select the seller outcome, comparable customer result and asset. The finished email should sound like one useful idea, not a research report.
